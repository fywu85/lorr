# Assessment of the Fable review

Fable 5.1 Max completed the requested independent pass through Claude Code. Its [review](review.md) is preserved separately from this assessment. The [metadata](metadata.json) records the explicit model and effort, successful completion, and returned usage. The CLI also recorded a small auxiliary Haiku call; the review response model was `claude-fable-5-1`.

The recommendation remains to repair dispatch while preserving persistent primaries and validated recovery witnesses. Fable adds a useful distinction: deadline exhaustion dominates initialization, while the fixed search-node limit drives many later fallback assignments.

**Findings checked against the code and records**

- Between the initial call and the timestep-800 diagnostic, the fixed SORTATION scheduler makes 1,846 fallback assignments out of 3,476 assignments, with zero additional deadline misses and 3,692 node-limit misses. The fallback-only prototype has 4,993 of 9,586 assignments through fallback, again with zero additional deadline misses and exactly twice as many node-limit misses. WAREHOUSE shows the same twice-per-fallback pattern, with about 35–36% of later assignments using fallback. Thus the problem persists after initialization. When an initial search finds no candidate, a second identical bounded search cannot discover a task after other assignments have only removed tasks; caching that failure can avoid redundant work. A search that found candidates subsequently taken by other robots is a different case and still needs replenishment.
- Both CITY-01 controls have zero fallback use in the recorded diagnostics. Its small fallback-only difference provides no positive evidence about sampling. RANDOM-01 also had no fallback exposure. The earlier holdouts establish limited non-regression where the fallback is unused.
- The simulator permits reassignment before the first stop, and the selected leader schedulers use it. NMS enables `ENABLE_SCHEDULER_CHANGE_TASK`; its inspected cost branches all use five times pickup distance plus chain cost. These are concrete generic design ideas to test, not evidence that copying the weight or re-matching will improve CGAR.
- The selected KittyKnight CMake defaults compile `wppl_planner`, and the NMS build comments out `Solution2`. The earlier review's `my_planner` and `Solution2` citations were implementation patterns from inactive code. They should not be used to attribute leader performance. The active snapshots also contain map-specific routing inputs that remain outside the user's requested scope. Their quantitative benefit and the attainable gap with generic routing remain unmeasured.
- The sequential GAME log shows one approximately 2,000-move recovery involving 66 robots occupying the transaction slot from before timestep 200 until after timestep 1,200. This supports a progress-latency concern. It does not isolate a throughput penalty. Improving dependency-aware execution while retaining and validating the complete witness is a separate later experiment.
- The sampler stride, 3,266,489,917, is prime. For the observed pool sizes at least 64, the 64 draws for one robot are distinct; repetition is only a small-pool issue here. The important outstanding problems are deadline accounting, structured sampling, and allocation quality, rather than duplicate draws in the large initial batches.

**Qualifications to Fable's interpretation and proposed patch**

The SORTATION fixed/hybrid/draft-scheduler controls share instrumented build v1, but the sampling runs use v2. The WAREHOUSE fixed and sampling runs share v2. Sampling also reserves a quarter of the scheduling budget, changing local search and allocation order. These are strong diagnostic results, but describing every sampling comparison as exactly one flag in an otherwise identical executable is too strong. Shortening local search is not guaranteed to bias the result against sampling; its contribution still needs an independent ablation.

The absence of later *routing* Manhattan fallbacks does not show that permanently cached approximate *task-chain* costs are minor. Those are different code paths and counters. Likewise, the hybrid holdouts do not isolate HRRN from distance-estimate inconsistency, changed matching, cache accesses, or time allocation. Disabling HRRN and testing globally weighted pickup costs are useful next ablations, with the separate fair admission retained and task-age tails measured.

Fable's first-patch list combines several independent changes. I would split it: time-accounted fallback quality and redundant-search avoidance first; shared discovery and resumable allocation next; cache policy and objective changes separately. Deferral is acceptable only with demonstrated progress across calls, including sparse endpoints, continuously arriving tasks, and budgets exhausted before admission. A stored cursor and random samples alone are not a deterministic coverage bound. Preserve existing single-call coverage tests for normal budgets and add distinct bounded-across-call tests for deliberately deferred work.

Re-matching must exclude the persistent primary and opened tasks, cap churn, validate eligibility, and preserve ticket semantics. Witness reordering needs independent action validation, including turns and reserved cells. Switching to the default planner is useful as a diagnostic control, but it does not preserve CGAR's recovery guarantees. These are proposals; no production changes or new ablation results are claimed in this review pass.

**Additional evidence from the requested parallel evaluation**

The [ten-way parallel benchmark](../../benchmarks/mr24-parallel-20260918/summary.md) ran while Fable reviewed the earlier records. It uses the same production executable as the sequential benchmark under the same one-CPU quota. All ten horizons complete without errors or timeouts, but the large cases lose throughput.

An additional offline analysis compares the first assignment of every GAME robot. All 6,500 robots receive their first task at step 1 in both runs. Mean first-pickup Manhattan distance rises from **2.76 to 284.10 cells**. The sequential run observes all 6,500 first pickups; the parallel run observes only 5,303 by step 5,000. This directly establishes a large initial allocation difference alongside the drop from 5,761 to 2,082 completed tasks. It does not isolate allocation from subsequent cache/routing effects or establish how the fallback prototype would behave in parallel. [Cohort data](../../benchmarks/mr24-parallel-20260918/game-initial-cohorts.json) and [analysis script](../../benchmarks/mr24-parallel-20260918/analyze_game_initial.py).

The production planner remains unchanged. The next implementation should improve assignment quality under computation pressure and test steady-state sparse-endpoint coverage, rather than pursuing wider scoring or map-specific routing as the immediate fix.
